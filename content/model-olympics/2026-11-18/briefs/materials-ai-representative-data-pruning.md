# What must a smaller teaching set preserve?

Report date: November 16, 2026. Paper date: October 19, 2026. Editorial edition: November 18, 2026.

Michigan Technological University describes research with UCLA on machine-learning prediction of electronic structure: how electrons are arranged and behave in materials. The training examples originate from Kohn–Sham density-functional-theory calculations. Removing examples before training differs from editing a trained model or conducting a physical materials experiment.

The university reports virtually unchanged prediction accuracy with 90% of training data removed, and most predictions still highly accurate with 99% removed. These percentages measure examples removed, not accuracy scores. Model training ran at least three times faster. The result does not measure whole discovery time, original calculation costs, energy, carbon, money or industrial production.

The accepted paper’s abstract describes useful random pruning as well as coverage-based sampling across learning difficulties. The latter almost always retains chemical accuracy and generalizability with up to two orders of magnitude less data. Neither method establishes a universal guarantee. Metals, water and complex alloys are reported examples; unfamiliar chemistries and conditions require further checks. The authors propose low intrinsic dimensionality as a geometric explanation, consistent with local-environment arguments.

The positive possibility is a more efficient computational research workflow, with practical gains still dependent on selection overhead, validation and subsequent experiments. Library examples and workflow arithmetic in the articles are explanatory illustrations, not additional study measurements.
