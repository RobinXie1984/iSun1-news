# Fifteen storage projects: awards, prototypes and the measurement ahead

Public source review, November9,2026. NYSERDA announced nearly$13million in awards to15energy-storage development and demonstration projects on November6. The public-benefit authority supports long-duration storage and advances beyond standard lithium-ion designs. The portfolio contains both lithium-based improvements and alternative approaches. Awards establish a funded development pathway, distinct from completed installations, actual disbursement or measured customer savings.

Examples illustrate different stages. Inlyte’s award of over$2.4million supports planned deployment of a more-than10hour iron-sodium battery. Alsym’s over$290,000 award supports design and testing of a10kWh sodium-ion prototype described by the agency as non-flammable. Aestus’s over$590,000 award supports an approximately35kWh ceramic heat-material test intended to inform a20MWh compressed-air storage design. Those two energy quantities refer to different stages, and neither supplies a power rating. The source also lists flow, zinc-based, portable and lithium-component projects.

Power measures the rate of delivery; energy measures the amount delivered or stored over time. Duration depends on both. Storage moves energy between times and incurs losses, so benefits depend on the charging source, location, dispatch and system conditions. The development work still needs appropriate measurements of usable capacity, safety, reliability, integration and cost before operational benefit can be assessed.
