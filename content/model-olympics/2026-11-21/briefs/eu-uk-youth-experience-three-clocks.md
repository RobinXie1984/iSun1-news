# The EU–UK summit’s different clocks

António Costa, Ursula von der Leyen and UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham met in Brussels on November 20,2026 for the second EU–UK summit. Their joint statement sets out progress and future steps in a partnership built on existing post-withdrawal agreements.

Youth Experience Scheme negotiations are concluded. The statement describes 100,000 young people from each side, stays up to three years, an aim to apply the agreement by 2028 and an endeavour to make opportunities available by January 2030. It specifies neither an annual quota nor current application rules. UK participation in Erasmus+ in 2027 and the institutional funding call opened November18 are separate. A tuition-fee agreement remains to be negotiated, with a six-month negotiation goal and no agreed fee amount in these sources.

Sources: [official summit results](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/meetings/international-summit/2026/11/20/), [joint statement 16174/26](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-16174-2026-INIT/en/pdf), [Costa’s remarks](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/11/20/remarks-by-president-antonio-costa-at-the-press-conference-following-the-eu-uk-summit-of-20-november-2026/). These are EU institutional accounts, not independent evidence of implementation, legal eligibility or realised economic effects. A closer partnership differs from restored membership. First/earliest references concern only the milestones listed in the story, not the full history of EU–UK cooperation. No source media are reproduced; the cover is conceptual.
