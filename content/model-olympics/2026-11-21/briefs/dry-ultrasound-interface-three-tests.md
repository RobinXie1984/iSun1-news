# A dry interface for ultrasound: three tests, three kinds of evidence

MIT News reported on November 19,2026 that MIT and SMART WITEC researchers developed a liquid-free, stretchy polymer interface for ultrasound. A bottlebrush molecular architecture and reinforcing second network address the tension between sound transmission and mechanical strength.

The reported tests have different settings and durations: around nine days of imaging through a synthetic tissue phantom; a ten-minute chest-worn treadmill sweat comparison; and a thirty-minute neck-patch cycling demonstration connected to a portable tablet. The nine-day result is not a nine-day human or exercise trial. Manufacturing work and possible use outside clinics remain prospective.

Sources: [MIT News report](https://news.mit.edu/2026/ultrasound-imaging-without-gel-1119), [NIH ultrasound background, updatedDecember 2023](https://www.nibib.nih.gov/science-education/science-topics/ultrasound), [identified Science paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aef9553).
