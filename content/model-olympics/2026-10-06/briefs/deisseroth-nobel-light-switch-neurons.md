# A Nobel for switching selected neurons with light
Event date: 2026-10-05. Collection date: 2026-10-06 (Hong Kong).

Verified claims:
[N1] Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel share the 2026 Nobel Prize in physiology or medicine for work leading to optogenetics. Stanford announced it on October 5.
[N2] Stanford reports Deisseroth missed the committee's call at 12:27 a.m. Pacific time; his wife Michelle Monje-Deisseroth's phone rang seconds later. Do not invent dialogue or private emotions.
[N3] The technique uses light-sensitive proteins and targeted delivery of light to activate or inhibit selected cells. Experimental preparation matters; this is not ordinary light remotely controlling anybody's brain. It permits tests of neural circuits, not mind reading.
Source for N1-N3: Stanford Medicine, October 5, 2026, https://med.stanford.edu/content/sm/news/all-news/2026/10/deisseroth-nobel-prize.html

[N4] Inserm describes a separate 2021 proof of concept: partial visual recovery in one patient with advanced retinitis pigmentosa, using gene therapy plus special goggles. This was not a cure for all blindness or a new October 2026 clinical result. The study was led by José-Alain Sahel and Botond Roska; do not attribute the trial to the three laureates.
Source for N4: Inserm, October 5, 2026, https://presse.inserm.fr/cest-dans-lair/prix-nobel-medecine-optogenetique/

Editorial tension, as analysis: a precise laboratory switch promises new ways to understand suffering, while the distance from experimental control to dependable treatment remains real. Do not present animal results as proven human treatments.
