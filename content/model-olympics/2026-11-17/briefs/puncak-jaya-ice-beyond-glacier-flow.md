# Ice remains as glacier flow dwindles

Report date: November 16, 2026. Collected November 17, Hong Kong time.

NASA Earth Observatory describes remnant ice on Puncak Jaya in Indonesia’s Sudirman Range, near the equator on New Guinea. Scientists distinguish the surviving stagnant ice from a functioning glacier system thick enough to flow under its own weight. The account does not say all mountain ice has vanished.

The article compares false-colour images dated November 3, 1988 and October 19, 2026. Those dates are verified in the primary page. Area estimates attributed to Donaldi Permana of Indonesia’s meteorological agency BMKG are 4.31 square kilometres in 1988 and 0.09 square kilometres in September 2025, around two percent of the earlier extent. The later image is not a new quantified total-area estimate. A December 2026 aerial survey is planned.

At one East Northwall Firn monitoring site, thickness was 32 metres in 2010 and four metres in 2023; the ice at that site was gone by November 2024. Those measurements are neither mountain-wide thickness nor total ice volume. Fragmentation, exposed dark rock and insufficient persistent snowfall help explain the decline. Forecasts of disappearance around 2030 or sooner remain attributed estimates, with El Niño an episodic amplifier of longer-term warming.

The 2019 Permana and colleagues paper provides historical context: two approximately 32-metre cores reached bedrock in 2010 and preserve information about earlier tropical Pacific variability.
