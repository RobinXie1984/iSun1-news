# Free allocation and the carbon-cost question

Source review, November 14, 2026. Council and Parliament negotiators reached a provisional agreement on November 12 to increase free allowances under EU ETS heat and fuel benchmarks for 2026–2030. Endorsement, legal-linguistic revision, formal adoption and Commission implementing rules remain subsequent steps. The roadmap's first-quarter 2027 timing is a target.

The proposed cushion is roughly 121 million allowances: around 88 million from an existing free-allocation pool, 28 million previously unallocated because installations had not met specified conditions, and a 5 million targeted set-aside. The set-aside is included in the total. Unused allowances from that targeted measure would support investment and innovation funds. These quantities establish neither annual cash grants, actual extra emissions, measured carbon savings nor a change in the overall emissions cap. The report identifies prior conditionalities; it does not establish a blanket waiver from every requirement.

Heat and fuel are fallback measurement approaches for products and processes outside suitable product benchmarks. They account for around 25% of free allocation, a different denominator from total emissions. Commission background distinguishes 52 product benchmarks and two fallback approaches. Carbon leakage describes a risk of relocation to jurisdictions with weaker carbon constraints; protecting competitiveness is a policy objective whose outcomes still need evidence. Actual eligibility, allocation, investment and emissions remain separate questions. Background pages contain pending-update notices, so unrelated proposed reforms and historical rates are excluded.
