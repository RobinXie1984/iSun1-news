# A data resource for the next cosmic map

Source review, November 14, 2026. ESA released Euclid's DR1-Foundation on November 12. It draws on around 13 months of observations, with its survey caption identifying spring 2024 to spring 2025. Earlier images and preliminary survey releases already existed. The new major release provides raw data, calibrated images, catalogues and spectra for subsequent research.

ESA reports more than a billion objects across 1,900 square degrees of Wide Survey sky, approximately 9,500 full-Moon apparent areas. This is about 14% of the intended survey footprint, rather than the whole sky. Reported galaxy, quasar-candidate, lens-candidate and spectral-source counts have different definitions and can overlap. The roughly 15,000 strong-lensing entries retain candidate status; more than 160 million spectra do not establish that many secure galaxy distances. The northern 367-square-degree, nearly 500-gigapixel mosaic is a subset.

Spectral features and redshift can support distance inference; lensing models connect distorted light with foreground mass. Both require validation and modelling. Dark matter and dark energy remain distinct unresolved questions. A fuller DR1 release is planned for mid-2027. This article inspected ESA reporting, rather than independently reanalysing the catalogues. Source images and videos remain links; the cover is a separately labelled AI-generated concept.
