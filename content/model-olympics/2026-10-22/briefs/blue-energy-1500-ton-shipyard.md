# Verified common brief — Blue Energy shipyard prefabrication
Prepared2026-10-22HKT. MIT News report dated2026-10-22:
https://news.mit.edu/2026/startup-rejuvenates-nuclear-power-industry-new-manufacturing-approach-1022
F1. Founder/CEO Jake Jurewicz studied nuclear engineering/physics at MIT and founded Blue Energy in2023. The company’s approach prefabricates large functional nuclear-plant blocks at shipyards and transports them by barge for assembly on-site, using established heavy fabrication workers/equipment and nuclear quality requirements. A1,500-ton block is not a complete working reactor, a floating nuclear plant, or permission to ignore licensing.
F2. The report gives block weight around1,500tons. Assembly over one year, roughly36month construction, revenue within three years and later-project cost around$5,000 per kilowatt are company design goals/projections. $/kW is a capacity-linked capital cost estimate, NOT cents/kWh/electricity price or proven final budget. No independently verified realized saving, completed plant, contracted risk allocation, licence number or construction start established here. Do not infer missing disclosures do not exist anywhere.
F3. Victoria,Texas first project is planned as a2.5GW hybrid plant:1GW using two GE Vernova gas turbines in2030, followed by up to1.5GW from up to five small modular reactors beginning2032.2030is NOT a confirmed nuclear-generation date. The gas phase emits carbon; nuclear operation has low direct operational carbon emissions but lifecycle zero-carbon and pollution-free claims are not established. Do not turn the planned2032sequence into guaranteed completed full nuclear conversion in2032.
F4. MIT reports NRC approval for the gas-to-nuclear approach, an agreement with GE Vernova Hitachi Nuclear Energy, and a reactor design already approved by the NRC. The first part of a Texas construction application was submitted inSeptember2026. Approval of approach/design, application submission, a full site construction licence and permission to operate are distinct. This brief does not independently inspect the NRC docket. Do not invent that the whole site is approved, nor assert categorically nothing is approved.
F5. Founder argues manufacturing yards can better manage fabrication risk than conventional one-off construction sites and help attract financing; lower risk/capital cost and local jobs/desalination/energy-security benefits are attributed expectations, not audited outcomes or signed risk-transfer terms. Positive angle: a useful manufacturing/financing approach directed at reliable energy deployment, with the honest tension of gas-first emissions, real construction evidence and future approvals. No invented site visit, barge delivery, workers’ emotions, named customers, power contracts or technical reactor recipe. Analysis must remain separate from reported claims.
