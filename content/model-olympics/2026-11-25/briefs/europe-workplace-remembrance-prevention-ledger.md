# Remembering workers, measuring prevention

Declaration report: 24 November 2026. First annual observance: 8 August 2027. Eurostat release: 25 September 2026; accident reference year: 2024.

The European Parliament, Council and Commission signed a declaration establishing an annual remembrance day for victims of accidents at work. Its date recalls the 8 August 1956 Bois du Cazier disaster in Marcinelle, Belgium, in which 262 miners died. Recognition, awareness and prevention commitments are distinct from new enforcement powers or a measured reduction in injuries.

Eurostat records 3,367 fatal accidents in 2024, up 45, and 2.78 million qualifying nonfatal accidents, down 1.5%. Fatal means death within one year of the accident; nonfatal means at least four full calendar days’ absence. These accident figures differ from the wider occupational-disease burden. Construction’s 776 fatal accidents represent 23.0% of the total, a count share rather than a per-worker risk ranking.

Administrative coverage, reporting incentives, workforce size and sector composition affect comparisons. Transparent definitions and comparable outcomes can make remembrance a useful checkpoint; the new day’s actual prevention effect remains unknown.
