# A giant black hole on a short cosmic clock

Report/publication metadata: 24 November 2026. Public author manuscript: 11 September 2026; final publisher text inaccessible.

ESA and MPIA report Euclid’s discovery of J1504+6252, nicknamed Dún Aonghasa, a spectroscopically confirmed luminous quasar at redshift 8.13. Its light records a cosmic age around 630 million years. The roughly 40-million-year advance concerns the earlier epoch reached by the quasar record, rather than the black hole’s own age or a record for every kind of celestial object.

The estimated central mass is roughly 360 million Suns. Gas spectra and luminosity calibrations carry substantial uncertainty. Its comparatively low inferred luminosity relative to the idealised Eddington limit describes the epoch observed; earlier growth remains inferred. Heavy seeds and faster or more sustained past feeding offer model-dependent pathways. Direct collapse or primordial origins have not been uniquely established.

The positive advance is observational: coordinated broad surveys and detailed spectroscopy create tighter tests of early black-hole growth. The owner has not independently reproduced the analysis.
