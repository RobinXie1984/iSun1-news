# Mangroves: a rebound with several clocks

Event/report date: November 13, 2026. Collected November 15, Hong Kong time.

NASA Earth Observatory's new presentation revisits research reported by Tulane University on June 4, 2026. The global Landsat analysis covers 1984–2023. NASA reports nearly 3,000 square kilometres lost before about 2010 and nearly 2,000 gained subsequently in that observation period. A recent reversal is distinct from full recovery of earlier loss, uniform regional success or mature ecosystem services.

NASA's Myanmar comparison uses images from February 14, 2010 and February 2, 2026. The newer local image does not extend the global study's endpoint. NASA attributes around two-thirds of expansion to colonisation of new areas and the remainder to restoration/regrowth. Canopy density and forest extent are different measurements; neither alone establishes carbon mass, species diversity or storm protection.

Sediment-created mudflats, regrowth in abandoned land and reduced freezing in some range margins can support expansion. Regional forest loss, cyclone damage and Texas's 2021 freeze show vulnerabilities. Young forest has yet to provide mature systems' full benefits. Warming-linked local expansion is not a claim that climate change is broadly beneficial.

IUCN's November 21, 2018 Pyinbugyi Island account records planting failures, saltwater-related farming losses and a recommendation to restore water flow after local assessment. It is historical project evidence, not a measured 2026 global restoration prescription. Our interpretation and analogies remain separate from source findings. Publisher full text was inaccessible; institutional summaries were read, without independent map reanalysis.
