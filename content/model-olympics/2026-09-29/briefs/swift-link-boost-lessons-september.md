# Swift and the servicing clock

Historical edition: September 29, 2026. Source report: NASA, September 28, updated September 29. New feature first published November 27, 2026.

NASA reports that Katalyst's LINK servicing mission ended with Swift's orbit unchanged. LINK launched in July after a roughly one-year development effort, experienced communication and orientation-control difficulties, and shifted to demonstration goals. NASA ended its involvement September 3; its account dates LINK's reentry to September 25. Swift was a separate older observatory, facing a projected later reentry in the September account.

Swift's Penn State operations team changed telescope orientations to reduce drag while respecting instrument-protection constraints. NASA describes several months gained above a difficult servicing threshold around 300 km, with a pause in pointed observations from mid-February through late August. This account distinguishes those operations and reported engineering lessons from an achieved orbital boost or a guaranteed future servicing capability.
