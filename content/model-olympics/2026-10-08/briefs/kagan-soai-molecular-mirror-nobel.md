# Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai win chemistry Nobel for molecular handedness
Event date: 2026-10-07. Collection date: 2026-10-08 (Hong Kong).

Verified source claims:
[C1] The American Chemical Society's October 7 announcement names Henri B. Kagan of Université Paris-Sud, Orsay, France, and Kenso Soai of Tokyo University of Science, Japan, as equal recipients of the 2026 chemistry Nobel. The work concerns non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis.
[C2] ACS explains Kagan helped establish principles for selective production of one molecular form, while Soai showed molecules can amplify their own handedness. Mirror-image molecular forms are not necessarily interchangeable in chemical interactions; do not generalise that every opposite form is toxic.
[C3] ACS reports Kagan has been a member for 60 years and Soai was a member for 26 years. Membership years do not establish the time each waited for a Nobel, their emotions, or the date of every discovery. Their seminal papers appeared in ACS journals.
Source C1-C3: American Chemical Society, October 7, https://www.acs.org/pressroom/newsreleases/2026/october/acs-president-comments-on-award-of-2026-nobel-prize-in-chemistry.html

Editorial tension (analysis): molecules that look like reflections can behave differently when they meet other molecules. Manufacturing a preferred handedness can matter to useful chemistry, while recognition names two people for different contributions. The prize is not a new medicine approval, a universal cure, or proof the origin of life's handedness is completely solved. No invented lab scene, quotation, patient outcome or age.
