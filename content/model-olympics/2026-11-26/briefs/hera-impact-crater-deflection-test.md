# Revisiting the asteroid humanity moved

Report date: 25 November 2026. Close-up observations: early November 2026. DART impact: 26 September 2022. Historical orbital analysis: March 2023.

ESA and DLR report a broad crater flattening one face of Dimorphos, chaotic tumbling and previously unknown metre-scale boulders around the Didymos–Dimorphos pair. Other regions retain boulder fields resembling the earlier DART images. The reports describe physical aftermath, rather than a new collision or an imminent threat to Earth.

DART demonstrated kinetic deflection in a controlled experiment. Hera’s next measurements of shape, mass and interior can help researchers calibrate how a different asteroid might respond. Plans for closer approaches and CubeSat deployment remain future activities. A successful experiment on one target does not establish a universal protective capability.

The positive advance is a new evidence chain connecting a measured orbital change to the physical body affected. This independent feature explains the mechanisms and limits; it is not a mission-team analysis or independent replication.
