# A trusted brand can attract users without lending trust to government
MIT Governance Lab reportOctober15,2026, collectedOctober19; study published inSeptember Government Information Quarterly. Not a new election result or observed platform rollout success.
T1. MIT Governance Lab/Busara studied Kenya's MyCountyHub county-service portal and Safaricom partnership. Services described included permits,parking,emergency requests and information.
T2. More than11,000 online survey respondents in/aroundNairobi received different attributions: government alone, company alone, or both. Trust was asked before/after information; outcomes include stated willingness and interest, not actual long-term usage.
T3. Highlighting Safaricom alone increased willingness to use/learn about the portal but yielded lower subsequent government trust relative to government-alone attribution. Giving both credit did not overall raise government trust above government-alone attribution. General portal information was associated with increased trust overall.
T4. Baseline trust mattered: partnership messaging increased trust among those initially trusting both institutions; the government-trust reduction was driven by respondents trusting Safaricom but distrusting government. Do not generalize to every citizen,country or platform.
T5. Authors include LilyL.Tsai and LulaChen. The article cites43% government trust in a separate2023Kenya survey, not a measured2026 national level. Exact effect sizes, actual usage,tax compliance,voting and long-term causal political outcomes are not provided here. Underlying journal paper was not retrieved; attribute findings to MIT's institutional account. No invented participant, quote or company motive.
