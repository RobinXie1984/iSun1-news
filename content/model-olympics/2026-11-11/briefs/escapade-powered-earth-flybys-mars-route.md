# A completed departure milestone, with Mars still ahead

Public source review, November11,2026. NASA’s November9report says both ESCAPADE spacecraft completed powered Earth flybys onNovember7and9. Rocket Lab’s November10contractor account reports the same departure milestone. The company built Blue and Gold for the NASA/University of California, Berkeley mission. These reports establish a mission-reported success, rather than this publication’s independent navigation-telemetry test.

NASA describes approximately9minute departure burns and closest-approach altitudes of about1000and650miles above Earth’s surface. Rocket Lab rounds the continuous burns to approximately10minutes. Each spacecraft combined the Earth encounter with its own propulsion. Autonomous execution had human operator oversight. The mission’s launch inNovember2025 led into a long loiter path around the Sun–EarthL2region before the pair returned to Earth in favorable geometry. The route demonstrates launch-planning flexibility while retaining fuel, navigation and planetary-alignment constraints.

A gravity assist exchanges energy and momentum with a moving planet in the relevant orbital frame. It creates no energy from nothing. ESCAPADE’s encounter also used engine thrust. The departure operation is distinct from the planned braking burns for Mars capture. NASA gives September7and9,2027 as planned insertion dates, followed by orbit adjustments and science operations expected fromJune2028. The paired observations aim to study solar wind interacting with Mars and driving atmospheric loss, first from one shared orbital path and later from separated locations. Earlier observations in Earth’s magnetotail are distinct from the future Mars campaign.

The positive advance is a completed demanding propulsion/navigation milestone after a deliberately long detour. Future capture, science results, astronaut-protection benefits and wider commercial economics remain separate questions. Performance percentages without defined denominators are excluded from this summary. The institutional and contractor accounts concern one mission, not independent replication.
