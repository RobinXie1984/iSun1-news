# Frozen factual brief — a proposed right to make one refusal last
Collection 2026-10-25; Council mandate agreed 2026-10-21, press release updated and mandate document dated 2026-10-22. This is a negotiating position, not enacted replacement law or a change already applying to Hong Kong websites.
Sources:
S1 https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/10/21/digital-rulebook-council-agrees-position-to-simplify-data-protection-privacy-and-cyber-reporting-rules/
S2 https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-14756-2026-INIT/en/pdf — mandate 14756/26, notably printed pages 137,157–158. Marked-up draft: deleted text must not be mistaken for the agreed Council wording. Printed page158 visually checked.
F1 Council agreed its position on the Digital Omnibus to simplify data protection, privacy and incident reporting. Negotiations with the European Parliament await Parliament's own position; no final agreement or implementation date established here. Existing obligations cannot be switched off on this basis. S1 and S2 cover.
F2 Proposed ePrivacy amendment would allow an easy, intelligible single-click refusal or equivalent means. After refusal, no new consent request for the same purpose for at least four months. This is not a four-month ban on all pop-ups, all cookies, or all communications, and not a new right already in effect. S2 printed page158.
F3 Council proposes clearer conditional cookie-consent exceptions for requested services, audience measurement and device security. The mandate specifies conditions on those uses. It does not legalise unrestricted tracking, profiling or sale of user information. Cookie provisions remain under the ePrivacy Directive. S1/S2 printed pages157–158.
F4 Council preserves the current definition of personal data, adds pseudonymisation/identifiability provisions and safeguards around proposed sensitive-data exceptions. It does not declare all pseudonymised data anonymous or give AI unrestricted access to personal data. S1.
F5 Council replaces the proposed EU-wide incident entry point with national entry points in each member state, and an EU-level information point. A proposed GDPR breach notification rule says without undue delay, where feasible no later than96 hours after awareness, for breaches likely to create a high risk to individuals' rights/freedoms. It does not grant a blanket96-hour waiting period or remove reporting obligations. S1/S2 printed page137.
F6 Ministerial claims about lower costs and stronger competitiveness are policy aims, not measured outcomes. Reducing repeat banners may improve an interface, but meaningful refusal, lawful exemptions and effective incident reporting create real tensions. No quantified savings or proven privacy outcome here. Discuss policy trade-offs as analysis, not legal advice; do not tell any business to change compliance today. S1/S2.
