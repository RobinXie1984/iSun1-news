# Factual brief — chemical 3D movies of living cells
Collection November1,2026. University of Tokyo report October30,2026: https://www.u-tokyo.ac.jp/focus/en/press/z0508_00481.html . Peer-reviewed primary paper published October30,2026: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-78699-z . Earlier March4 preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03812 . The university report and paper describe the same research; they are complementary source records. Current collection is a November1 selection of October30 research.
F1 Background/bigger picture: cells contain moving molecules and structures; a still picture can miss their changing spatial relationships. The University of Tokyo team includes first author/doctoral researcher Masato Fukushima, Keiichiro Toda, Yusei Sugawara, Shotaro Kawano and senior author Takuro Ideguchi. The method is mid-infrared photothermal optical diffraction tomography (MIP-ODT). Explain essential terms in everyday language, using a clearly illustrative analogy rather than an invented lab scene.
F2 Mid-infrared light excites characteristic molecular vibrations, causing small heat-related optical changes. Visible probing measures those changes; images with infrared illumination on/off and multiple viewing angles reconstruct chemically specific three-dimensional volumes. Eleven illumination angles and a spatial light modulator that rapidly shapes/steers light help achieve video-rate capture with strong single-shot signals. The apparatus records optical/photothermal signatures; talk of temperature signatures describes inference from these signals.

F4 Demonstration: researchers tracked lipid droplets (intracellular fat stores with metabolic roles) moving in three dimensions in living cultured COS-7 cells,including spatially varied transport. The paper also includes fixed-cell chemical imaging; distinguish those experiments from live-cell tracking. Dyes are unnecessary for the chemical contrast used here. Label-free describes the contrast mechanism; biological effects of illumination still warrant evaluation. The demonstrated single-cell sample thicknesses range from several micrometres to about10micrometres; a micrometre is one-millionth of a metre. Deeper tissues face water absorption,light scattering and reconstruction limitations. Observing a whole body or diagnosing/treating disease exceeds the demonstrated result.
