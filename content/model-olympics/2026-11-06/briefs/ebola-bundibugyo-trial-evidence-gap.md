# Bundibugyo: outbreak counts and the next evidence step

Public source review, November6,2026. WHO’s November4 recommendation would add Oxford/Serum Institute of India ChAdOx1 and Moderna mRNA-1469 to the DRC’s Tumaini trial after national approvals. These are candidates whose efficacy against Bundibugyo remains to be measured. WHO reports four vaccination rings and forecasts vaccine results in early2027. A recommendation, a ring count and a forecast each establish a different stage; none establishes an efficacy percentage. Treatment research runs alongside vaccine research, with different prevention/survival questions.

ECDC’s page, updated November5 at16:00 as displayed, reports 10,668 confirmed DRC cases including5,198 related deaths, using data through November3. These are dated reported surveillance totals. Kenya’s9confirmed cases/2deaths have an October26 cutoff; Nigeria’s62contacts under follow-up have a November3 cutoff. Uganda’s outbreak had ended according to that snapshot. These counts must retain their individual dates and cannot be converted into current uniform regional totals or trial outcomes.

The four DRC-linked cases evacuated/imported to the EU/EEA sinceMay have their own geographic scope. Switzerland’s later medical transfer is outside that EU/EEA total. A medical evacuation does not itself establish uncontrolled community transmission. ECDC’s low/very-low risk estimates are attributed geographic assessments subject to review, distinct from the outbreak’s severe local burden.

CDC’s June2 background distinguishes Bundibugyo from Zaire ebolavirus. Ervebo and the cited FDA-approved Ebola treatments target Zaire species; those approvals do not establish efficacy against Bundibugyo. Symptoms typically arise2–21days after exposure; infected body fluids/contaminated objects and care-related contact are the transmission context described by CDC. Ordinary proximity in public alone is not that exposure route. Diagnosis, supportive care, infection control and contact tracing continue while trial evidence is gathered. The article offers research reporting, not individualized care or travel directions.
