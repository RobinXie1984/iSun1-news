# EU methane deadlines: a negotiating position, not an enacted extension

Public source review, November 7, 2026. On November 6 the Council of the EU agreed its position on the Commission’s October 14 proposal to amend Regulation (EU) 2024/1787. The Council represents national governments; the European Parliament is the other legislator. Agreement with Parliament and final legislation remain necessary. Current targeted import-related monitoring rules were scheduled to apply on January 1, 2027 in the Council’s announcement.

The Council would move three different clocks by one year: import-related monitoring/reporting/verification from January 1, 2027 to January 1, 2028; methane-intensity reporting for EU producers/importers from August 5, 2028 to August 5, 2029; and maximum methane-intensity levels from August 5, 2030 to August 5, 2031. Application, submission of evidence and compliance with a limit are different requirements.

The mandate’s proposed Article 28 also sets first importer compliance reporting by May 31, 2029, using previous-calendar-year information, alongside January 1, 2028 application for relevant newer contracts. Older contracts have reasonable-efforts provisions. May31 and January1 must not be used as interchangeable deadlines.

Parliament’s October 22 position, adopted 341 votes to 225 with 6 abstentions and referred back for negotiations, seeks longer reporting delays. Its MRV-equivalence reporting date is May 31, 2031 rather than May 31, 2029 in the proposal; methane-intensity reporting would begin August 5, 2031 rather than August 5, 2029. Those comparisons concern the proposal and separate negotiating positions, not a final compromise or identical current-law deadlines.

The Council mandate proposes a Commission readiness assessment by September 30, 2027, with another by June 30, 2028 after a negative prior outcome. It would examine equivalent verification rules, methods for information transfer between producer/importer, sufficient independent verifiers and third-country equivalence procedures. If required conditions are missing, the draft provides for a delegated act postponing listed obligations another year. The text also addresses possible further proposed changes. These are proposed future mechanisms, not assessments already completed or automatic repeal of the whole regulation.

The Council attributes its approach to supply-security pressure amid Middle East energy-market disruption. The supplied primary documents do not establish actual price relief, a measured compliance-cost saving or a quantified climate effect of postponement. The Commission’s existing Q&A explains why evidence must pass through complex supply chains and why general compliance documents do not replace every required source/verifier record.

The 10-page mandate was extracted and visually checked. Its final page strikes out the word “twentieth” before the day-following-publication clause. Plain extraction retains that deleted word, so it cannot support a new 20-day entry rule. No amendment publication or final entry date is verified here.
