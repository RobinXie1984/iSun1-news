The Rain Came, the Lead Vanished, and Then Max Verstappen Did What Champions Do
A champion's long wait ends in the Sepang downpour — but nothing about this victory was straightforward

Edition date: October 5, 2026 | Race date: October 4

There is a particular kind of silence that follows a four-time world champion when he hasn't won a race in months. It's not the absence of noise. It's the presence of questions. The kind that don't get asked out loud but hang in every paddock conversation, every post-race debrief, every headline that uses the word "drought."

On Sunday, October 4, at the Sepang International Circuit, that silence ended. But it didn't end cleanly. It never does.

Max Verstappen won the 2026 Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia — yes, you read that correctly, and no, that's not a typo — and in doing so, delivered the first victory of the season for both himself and Red Bull Racing. The FIA's official report describes it as the maiden triumph for Red Bull Ford Powertrains.-
1
 That's a milestone with weight. A new power unit partnership, a new season, a champion who had been waiting.

But here's what the headline won't tell you: even pole position didn't make this straightforward.

The Wait

Let's set the scene properly. This is not Verstappen's first career win. He is a four-time world champion. He knows what it feels like to cross the line first. The difference is that in 2026, he hadn't felt it yet. Not until Sepang.

The F1 report notes that Verstappen claimed his and Red Bull's first pole position of the 2026 season in qualifying, edging out Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton, with teammate Isack Hadjar rounding out the top three before a five-place grid penalty dropped him back.-
2
 That penalty — for using extra power unit elements — would prove to be a small thread in a much larger tapestry of chaos.

Wait, let me correct myself. Not chaos. Weather.

The Rain

In the hour before lights out, a heavy rain shower — accompanied by a thunderstorm — soaked the Sepang circuit.-
2
 If you know anything about Sepang, you know what that means. This is a track that doesn't do "light drizzle." It does monsoons.

The race was scheduled for 1500 local time. Race Control confirmed a delay just minutes before the start, with teams sheltering their cars on the grid under gazebos while the rain continued to pour.-
2
 An exploratory lap by the Medical Car illustrated just how treacherous the conditions had become — it slid its way around the track and reported significant standing water.-
2

The FIA report states that a heavy downpour delayed the scheduled start by 40 minutes.-
1
 But that was just the first delay. What followed was something stranger — something that turned a routine wet race into a test of procedure, patience, and the kind of decision-making that separates champions from contenders.

The False Start

The field eventually moved away from their grid slots on intermediate tyres — some 40 minutes later than planned — for what was supposed to be a formation lap followed by a standing start.-
2
 There would be a minimum of two laps behind the Safety Car before the standing start, subject to change based on driver feedback.-
2

Then things got weird.

Hadjar lost control exiting Turn 2 and took a trip through the gravel. Verstappen reported power issues. "I can't accelerate! It's not working, guys," he said over the radio.-
2
 Hamilton, meanwhile, reported: "I'm pressing the throttle and nothing's happening!" before grinding to a halt on the start/finish straight.-
2
 Ferrari told him it was a software issue they were trying to solve.-
2

With more cars encountering problems and lapping much more slowly than usual, Race Control suspended the starting procedure and threw a red flag. The drivers returned to the pit lane. A second attempt at a start was scheduled for 1633 — 93 minutes after the original start time.-
2

The FIA report describes it as an "interrupted start procedure" that caused another delay.-
1
 The understatement is almost poetic.

The Start

When the lights finally went out, the race that unfolded was worth the wait. And it immediately went wrong for the polesitter.

Antonelli and Russell made excellent starts from the second row to sweep past Verstappen and establish a Mercedes one-two into Turn 1.-
1
 The Red Bull driver narrowly retained third from a fast-starting Liam Lawson.-
1
 The FIA report notes that nine drivers opted for intermediate tyres while the remainder gambled on slicks — Verstappen, Antonelli, Russell, and Hadjar among the former, Hamilton, Leclerc, Norris, and Piastri among the latter.-
1

The contrasting strategies immediately began to shape the race. The Ferraris and Norris struggled to make progress on slick tyres, while Leclerc made contact with Hülkenberg's Audi, forcing an early pit stop for a new front wing and a switch to intermediates.-
1

Verstappen, meanwhile, began his recovery. On Lap 6, he passed Russell around the outside into Turn 4 to reclaim second place.-
1
 By Lap 8, he had closed on Antonelli and was applying pressure to the race leader.-
1

Then Bottas lost control through Turn 13 and became stranded in the gravel. Safety Car.-

The Decision

Here is where the race turned — not definitively, not provably, but undeniably. The Safety Car provided an ideal opportunity for the intermediate runners to switch to slick tyres, which had become the faster compound as the circuit dried.-
1
 Verstappen, Antonelli, Russell, and Hadjar all took advantage.-
1

But the tyre choices diverged. Mercedes opted for Medium tyres. Red Bull selected Softs for both Verstappen and Hadjar.-
1

I want to be careful here. The FIA report notes that this "set up a potentially decisive difference in pace as the race resumed."-
1
 But it also implicitly acknowledges that these are observed race decisions — not proof that one tyre choice alone caused the victory. Anyone who has watched a wet-to-dry race knows that track position, driver confidence, traffic, and a dozen other variables matter just as much as compound selection.

What happened next, though, was unambiguous.

When the race resumed, Verstappen wasted little time. He swept around the outside of Antonelli into Turn 1 to take the lead.-
1
 Russell then passed his teammate at Turn 5, dropping Antonelli to third within moments of the restart.-
1
 Verstappen began to establish a gap, moving more than three seconds clear of the Mercedes pair.-
1

The champion was in front. And he was pulling away.

The Recovery

If Verstappen's story was one of reclamation, Hamilton's was one of resurrection.

The seven-time champion had started on slick tyres and paid the price. By the time the Safety Car period ended, he was massively out of position, tumbling down the order as the intermediate runners gained traction.-
2
 Ferrari kept him out on slicks, engaging in what the F1 report describes as "a bit of back-and-forth" with the pit wall before settling on sticking with their "gamble."-
2

But Hamilton's recovery gathered momentum. Having fallen towards the rear of the field, he climbed to fifth by Lap 22, overtaking several rivals in quick succession. Leclerc followed, breaking into the top 10 one lap later.-
1

Meanwhile, Antonelli's challenge was briefly compromised on Lap 24 when he ran wide at the final corner and struck the pit-entry bollard, losing valuable time. The gap to Verstappen increased to 7.5 seconds.-

Then came the second round of pit stops. Russell entered on Lap 32 to switch to Soft tyres, expressing concern that the stop had come too early. Antonelli pitted one lap later to cover the undercut threat. Verstappen followed, retaining a comfortable advantage of approximately 11 seconds over the Mercedes.-
1

Hamilton kept climbing. By the time the late-race interruptions arrived, he was in contention for a podium.-

The Late Chaos

Alex Albon stopped with what the FIA report describes as a gearbox issue on Lap 41, bringing out a Virtual Safety Car.-
1
 Verstappen took advantage of the reduced-speed conditions to make another pit stop, temporarily surrendering the lead to Antonelli. Both Ferraris also changed tyres, while Mercedes brought both cars in on the following lap.-
1

Then the Virtual Safety Car was replaced by a full Safety Car, neutralising the field and creating a final opportunity for teams to adjust their strategies. Verstappen returned to the front ahead of Antonelli and Russell, with Hadjar fourth and Hamilton fifth.-
1

The final restart set up a short but decisive run to the chequered flag. Verstappen maintained control at the front. Hamilton and Leclerc completed their recovery drives by passing Hadjar to move into third and fourth respectively.-

And then, with just a handful of laps remaining, George Russell's Mercedes ground to a halt on the start/finish straight after completing 49 laps.-

I will not speculate on the cause of Russell's failure. The sources do not provide a reason, and inventing one would be irresponsible. What is known is this: Russell retired. Antonelli, who was already set to extend his championship lead over his teammate, gained an additional advantage in the Drivers' Championship.-

What This Means (And What It Doesn't)

Here is where I need to separate what happened from what might happen.

What happened: Max Verstappen won a Grand Prix in 2026. Red Bull Ford Powertrains won its first race. A champion who had been waiting for months got his season back on track.

What this doesn't prove: That Verstappen is now on an unstoppable championship comeback. That the Soft tyre choice was definitively the right call. That Red Bull Ford has suddenly found a permanent engineering advantage over Mercedes.

The FIA report is careful to note that the tyre strategy "set up a potentially decisive difference in pace" — but "potentially" is doing a lot of work in that sentence.-
1
 Mercedes chose Mediums. Red Bull chose Softs. Verstappen made the move stick. Antonelli pushed him all the way to the line. The margin was 2.307 seconds. This was not a dominant victory. It was a won victory — earned, contested, and ultimately claimed by the driver who made the fewest mistakes when it mattered most.

And that, perhaps, is the most interesting thing about it.

The Champion's Silence

Verstappen has won four world championships. He knows what it takes. He also knows that the difference between winning and losing in Formula 1 is often measured in moments — a pit stop here, a tyre choice there, a Safety Car that arrives at the right time or the wrong one.

On Sunday at Sepang, the moments went his way. But they didn't go his way by accident. He lost the lead at the start and took it back. He managed the gap through two Safety Car periods. He kept his head when the race was red-flagged before it had even begun. He drove the kind of race that wins championships, even if it doesn't guarantee them.

The wait is over. The questions about a winless season are answered. What comes next is unknown.

One More Thing

Before I close, a note on the location — because this has confused people.

The race was the Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia, held at the Sepang International Circuit. This is not a mistake. F1 and the FIA confirmed on July 26, 2026, that Malaysia would join the 2026 calendar as the host venue for the Bahrain Grand Prix. It's an unusual arrangement, but it's the official one. The event is not being relocated to Bahrain, and July's announcement is not new. It's simply the context for why the race was called the "Bahrain Grand Prix" while taking place in Kuala Lumpur.-
