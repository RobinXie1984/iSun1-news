From an analytical standpoint, this split strategy highlights a classic F1 compromise: immediate grip versus long-term durability. The Soft tyre warms up faster—a critical advantage behind the slow pace of a Safety Car on a damp-but-drying track. The Medium tyre takes longer to generate temperature but inherently suffers less thermal degradation over a long stint.

We cannot state as an empirical fact that the tyre choice alone won Red Bull the race. Victory in F1 is a complex matrix of car performance, tyre delta, track position, and driver execution. However, the observable race decisions suggest Red Bull prioritized the immediate restart, while Mercedes took a slightly more conservative, long-view approach.
