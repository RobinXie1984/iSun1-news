The Matrix of Valid Votes
Brazilian electoral law operates on the principle of votos válidos—valid votes. Blank votes, null votes, and spoiled ballots are entirely discarded from the final calculus. A candidate does not need 50% of all registered voters, nor 50% of all humans who walked into a voting booth; they need 50% plus one of the valid votes cast to avoid a runoff.
At the 99.99% counting snapshot, the TSE reported Flav Flavio Bolsonaro secured 47.03% of the valid votes, while Lula secured 45.16%.
This leaves a gap of 1.87 percentage points.
It is vital to distinguish the nature of this gap. This 1.87 figure is the absolute subtraction of their published vote shares (47.03 minus 45.16). It is a measurement of the electoral landscape's tilt. It is not a 1.87% relative lead. In the language of statistics, a relative lead would measure the size of the gap against the trailing candidate's total, which would yield a different, larger percentage. The TSE's data provides the absolute percentage-point spread, offering a clear, unvarnished view of how the valid electorate distributed its support between the top two contenders.

The math dictates that neither man crossed the 50% threshold of valid votes. Therefore, the first round has fulfilled its constitutional purpose: it has narrowed the field. It has not, however, decided the president.
