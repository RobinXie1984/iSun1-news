# Europe's promises and payments run on different clocks

Source review, November 12, 2026. Council and Parliament negotiators reached a provisional agreement on the EU's 2027 annual budget on November 11. It is the final annual budget of the 2021–2027 multiannual framework. Both institutions still need to formally approve it. Council endorsement is expected November 23; this is a future procedural step.

The rounded totals are €199.7 billion in commitments and €211.5 billion in payments. Commitments authorise obligations whose activities may extend across years. Payments cover spending from current and preceding years' commitments. Adding the figures would double-count different accounting measures; their difference does not establish a deficit. Annual revenue and expenditure must balance under the Council's explanation.

The agreement leaves €710.1 million under spending ceilings for unforeseen needs. That margin is distinct from a bank balance or the entirety of emergency resources. The table's major commitment headings include cohesion, resilience and values (€75.8 billion) and natural resources and environment (€57.1 billion), rounded from amounts given in millions. They measure the shared EU layer, not total national public spending or delivered outcomes.

Ireland chaired Council negotiations led by Simon Harris; the Commission facilitated talks with Parliament. Parliament's October 21 position provides negotiating history, and its requested increases must remain separate from final allocations. Budget approval, implementation and later audit are successive tests. This publication observed institutional documents, not beneficiary payments or future project performance.
