# Reprogramming immune cells inside the body — a mouse-study advance
MIT News, October 13, 2026; collected October 18. Not breaking today.
K1. MIT reports ligand-directed lipid nanoparticles carrying mRNA can engineer CAR natural killer (NK) cells in vivo, potentially avoiding external cell manufacture. Daniel Anderson is senior author; Yuang Chen, Julia Monkovic and Haseeb Mughal lead authors; Robert Langer is a co-author.
K2. In mice, about 80% of targeted NK cells were reprogrammed. In one aggressive melanoma mouse model, three of five treated mice entered complete remission. That small group is not a human success rate.
K3. In a humanized-mouse T-cell lymphoma model, tumors were eradicated after five injections. In an autoimmune-hepatitis mouse model, one injection destroyed autoreactive T cells and liver damage healed, MIT reports.
K4. Potential advantages concern manufacturing complexity and cost; no human clinical effectiveness, safety, actual price, durability or availability is established here. MIT says no CAR NK therapies are FDA-approved at the report date. Animal remission is not a cure for patients.
K5. The linked Nature Biotechnology paper was not independently retrieved; this brief attributes results to MIT's institutional report. Funding includes Sanofi, Marble Center and an NCI grant. No invented patient, quotation or clinic.
