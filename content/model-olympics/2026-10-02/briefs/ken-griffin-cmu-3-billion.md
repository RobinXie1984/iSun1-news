# Ken Griffin commits $3 billion to CMU, including a new Miami campus
Announcement date: September 30, 2026. Verified October 2, 2026 Hong Kong time.

[c1 / s1,s2] Carnegie Mellon University and Citadel founder and CEO Ken Griffin announced a $3 billion philanthropic gift/commitment: $2 billion for a new CMU Miami campus and $1 billion for Pittsburgh. The Pittsburgh share is split evenly between university priorities and $500 million for its School of Computer Science, renamed the Kenneth C. Griffin School of Computer Science. This is a gift, not a purchase of the university.
[c2 / s1] Griffin will join CMU's Board of Trustees. The announcement does not establish control over admissions, hiring or research conclusions.
[c3 / s1] CMU plans to organize the Miami campus around societal challenges rather than academic majors alone, initially including health/biology, national security, energy/climate and advanced manufacturing. This is the university's proposed education model, not proven superior outcomes or the elimination of all disciplines.
[c4 / s1] The campus is planned for more than 35 acres in Miami's Wynwood neighborhood. Construction is slated for 2027 and first enrollment planned for 2028, pending regulatory approvals. Once complete, it is intended to support more than 3,500 students, nearly 300 faculty and over 600 staff. These are plans, not people already enrolled or hired.
[c5 / s1,s2] CMU calls the commitment the largest individual gift in higher-education history. Attribute that superlative to CMU; no independent historical ranking was established in this brief.
