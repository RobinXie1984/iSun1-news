# A recurring star and evidence for a compact companion

Public source review, November10,2026. NASA and the Chandra X-ray Center reported a study of AT2016blu on November9. The luminous blue variable in NGC4559, roughly30million light-years away, has an estimated mass of at least33solar masses. The author preprint reports27detected quasi-periodic optical outbursts between2012 andJune2026, with an approximate113day recurrence. Detected events, observing gaps and variable peak timing remain distinct from a perfectly periodic complete census.

Three archival Chandra observations from2001–2002 yielded an X-ray upper-limit constraint. Five closely spaced observations onMarch24–27,2026 detected X-rays during an optical outburst. The paper compares the new luminosity with the earlier upper limit and reports a roughly100-fold increase; NASA describes the new emission as at least100times brighter. This is an X-ray comparison against a constraint, rather than two exact detections or a measured switch-on during March. Chandra’s angular resolution helps separate the system from a nearby bright X-ray source.

The favored interpretation is an eccentric binary with a compact companion, either a neutron star or stellar-mass black hole. Material expelled by the massive star may be captured more readily near closest approach. Accretion heats matter outside the compact object, while surrounding gas can reprocess energetic radiation into optical light. The orbit, gas flow and companion identity remain inferred or unresolved. The novelty concerns this LBV supernova-impostor class and intermittent accretion, rather than the first X-rays from any variable star. No future eruption date or danger to Earth follows from this study.
