# The strawberry with more fruit and a harder space problem

NASA report: November 25, 2026. Editorial date and first publication: November 27, 2026. NASA summarises strawberry and cucumber papers published in September and August respectively.

NASA Kennedy researchers and collaborators at the University of Melbourne and University of Western Australia compared seed-grown wild strawberries with Delizz. NASA reports twice as many buds, flowers and fruits on the wild plants, whose smaller fruit and spreading runners complicate the comparison. Delizz remained the leading candidate. Counts and edible mass are distinct measures; crop value also depends on the growing space and care required.

Cucumber findings are separately described as NASA-funded work, with steady edible-fruit production under flight-like testing. Lemon basil has completed preliminary testing and seed sanitisation; growth in plant pillows is the next stated assessment. These candidate stages differ from orbital validation and food production on future Moon/Mars missions. Existing Veggie and Advanced Plant Habitat systems provide the engineering background. Ohalo III is a planned prototype investigation in the November account.
