A marathon requires a runner to cover 42.195 kilometers, or about 26.2 miles, on foot. On November 1, 2026, the New York Road Runners, a nonprofit race organizer, held the New York City Marathon. The race began in 1970, and 2026 marks fifty years of the current course routing through all five boroughs of the city. According to the organizer's approximate count, nearly 60,000 athletes started the race. The final count of verified finishers awaits the organization's November 2 release.

Moving from shorter running events to a marathon requires a deep physical adjustment. Imagine a chef who has perfected small, delicate pastries deciding to bake a towering, multi-tiered wedding cake. The basic ingredients remain identical, while pacing the work and supporting the massive structure demand a completely different approach. Sweden's Andreas Almgren faced a similar transition. He entered the race as the 2026 World Athletics Half Marathon champion and the European 10,000-meter champion. On this Sunday, he attempted the full marathon distance for the first time.

Almgren stayed with the lead pack of runners through 25 miles of the course. With one mile remaining, he made a decisive acceleration away from the group to win the men's open division. The organizer identifies him as the first open-division male to win his debut marathon in New York since 2010, and the first athlete from Sweden to win any division in the event. Italy's Yeman Crippa finished in second place. Kamohelo Mofolo of Lesotho claimed third place on his twenty-first birthday, achieving the first podium finish for his country in the history of the race.

Kenya's Sheila Chepkirui won the women's open division with a time of 2 hours, 24 minutes, and 36 seconds, securing her second title following a win in 2024. American Jess McClain took second place, finishing ahead of Hellen Obiri.
