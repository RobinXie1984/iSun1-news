# Coral identities: old references and modern evidence

Research/report date: November 12, 2026. Collected November 16, Hong Kong time.

James Cook University reports research led by Augustine Crosbie with Queensland Museum. The team reassessed 53 named Acropora corals, a genus including branching and table forms. Forty names have eastern Australian type localities, with 13 additional Indo-Pacific names included for comparison. Forty-seven species are supported as distinct; the twelve removed from synonymy and four formerly unresolved species are included in that total.

Museum type specimens anchor names. Modern matching specimens, drawn from original localities or the best attainable related provenance, help connect skeletal characters with genomic lineages. The paper’s indexed methods recognise differing geographic confidence and treat reference matches as testable hypotheses. Old skeletons and modern genetic samples play different roles.

The advance concerns recognising existing diversity. A better inventory can inform monitoring and conservation decisions; it does not demonstrate population growth, reef recovery or new extinction-risk classifications. Explanatory analogies and practical implications in the articles remain analysis.
