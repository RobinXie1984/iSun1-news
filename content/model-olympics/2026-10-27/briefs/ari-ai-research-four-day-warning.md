# Frozen factual brief — proposed reporting on AI research automation
Collection 2026-10-27. Proposal covered by Nextgov on 2026-10-26; ARI's underlying blueprint was released earlier in 2026. Not an enacted rule or a new government office already operating.
Primary: https://ari.us/policy-bytes/keeping-pace-with-the-frontier-an-early-warning-system-for-accelerated-ai-rd-automation/
Primary earlier blueprint: https://ari.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/26.08.06-Frontier-AI-Framework-FINAL.pdf
F1 Americans for Responsible Innovation (ARI), an advocacy organization, proposes a Commerce office receiving confidential quarterly filings from major frontier developers on AI R&D automation. Congress would need to establish the system; adoption is UNKNOWN.
F2 Four proposed reporting categories: data/training inputs; algorithmic/architectural design; experimentation/research direction; safety/evaluation/oversight. Measures would include autonomous compute share and human review of AI-generated inputs/decisions; detailed fields would be set through rulemaking.
F3 Material shifts would trigger notification within four BUSINESS days of discovery, or when monitoring should have detected them. Possible triggers include a fully automated research loop or a new architecture autonomously performing a covered activity. Routine quarterly filings and this faster track serve different purposes.
F4 Under the proposal, filings would be confidential and exempt from FOIA, with aggregate industry reports for the public. The office could ask follow-up questions, increase reporting cadence and refer knowingly false/incomplete filings to DOJ; a whistleblower program is proposed. These powers do not exist merely because ARI requests them.
F5 Earlier blueprint proposes coverage requiring both at least 10^26 training FLOP and at least $100million aggregate AI-training spend in the past year; these are proposed scope tests, not enacted thresholds. It argues for standards, assurance and transparency, including internal models. This background predates the October26 news.
F6 Tension: automation may accelerate development beyond oversight; magnitude remains uncertain. Reporting can improve visibility but is not proof of safety. The costs, implementation, comparability, protection of confidential information and independent verification remain questions for analysis, not established outcomes. Omit unverified company-percentage claims, incident allegations and forecasts. No invented quotes, private emotions or scenes. No claim AI already autonomously builds all successors, that a four-day reporting clock is a development deadline, or that ordinary users must report their chats.
