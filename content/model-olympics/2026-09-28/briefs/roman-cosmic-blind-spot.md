NASA's launch release records that the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope launched on August 30, 2026. At launch, NASA forecast first images in early 2027 and roughly 1.4 terabytes of data per day. Those were forward-looking expectations in the published episode. The story record was checked on September 20; it does not establish that the forecast images, data output or discoveries had already arrived. [c1; s1]

Roman and Hubble have primary mirrors of equal diameter. Roman's wide-field instrument covers about 100 times the sky area per image. NASA says Hubble has observed one-tenth of one percent of the sky after more than three decades. These comparisons concern survey coverage and instrument field of view; they do not mean Roman's primary mirror is 100 times larger, or establish a corresponding multiplication of every measure of scientific performance. [c2, c6; s2]

Roman's planned wide-area survey targets more than a billion galaxies to investigate dark matter and the accelerating expansion of the universe. Astronomer David Weinberg estimates that displaying the complete planned high-latitude survey at full resolution would require half a million 4K televisions. This is a comparison for the scale of future survey output. The record does not describe an existing map or a photograph already assembled from the mission. [c3, c7; s3]

A separate NASA account forecasts roughly 100,000 transiting planets, and says Roman data will be public. Projected planet yields remain predictions, not discoveries. NASA also describes Roman's broad surveys and Webb's detailed infrared observations as complementary scientific roles. [c4–c5; s4–s5]

The sources therefore contain several different timelines: the August 30 launch, earlier descriptions of planned science, and expected observations after launch. The published episode's quantitative forecasts remain attached to those original announcements, with no September 28 mission-status update inferred from preparing this brief.
