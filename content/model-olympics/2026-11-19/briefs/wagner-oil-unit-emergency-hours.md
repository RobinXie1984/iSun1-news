# A runtime limit and a reliability decision

Order issued: November 17, 2026. Effective: November 18–December 31, 2026. Editorial edition: November 19, 2026.

The U.S. Department of Energy renewed conditional authority concerning oil-fired H.A. Wagner Unit 4 in Anne Arundel County, Maryland. PJM coordinates regional grid operation; Talen’s subsidiary owns and operates the unit. Its roughly 397 MW summer rating is power capacity. The 438-hour annual fuel-oil operating limit is a regulatory constraint; PJM’s October 23 estimate of zero remaining allowance is neither an empty fuel tank nor proof of zero physical capability.

The order retains two dispatch routes: declared or anticipated generation/transmission alerts, and dispatch consistent with the FERC-accepted settlement. The application’s settlement excerpt includes specified reliability/restoration/capacity needs and required testing. An alert-only summary would omit this second route. The order requires necessary-hours/cost controls, cessation consistent with operating parameters, following-business-day notice when used, and retained environmental obligations. Emission fees, offsets or allowances receive no blanket exemption. Expiry is distinct from applicable continuing environmental-information obligations.

The application attributes concern to transmission constraints and generation availability. Intended retirements and projected reliability risks differ from observed outages. Temporary authority does not establish actual generation, savings, emissions, avoided blackouts or completed grid upgrades. The order notes litigation over statutory authority and states DOE’s position; the broader court record was not independently examined. Any longer-term options in the articles are analysis, not approved substitutes or legal/investment advice.
