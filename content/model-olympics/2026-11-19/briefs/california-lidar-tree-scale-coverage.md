# A detailed California map with dated observations

NASA report: November 18, 2026. Dataset release: October 13. Survey acquisitions: 2013–2023. November 19 archive addition first published November 27, 2026.

Researchers used about 40 TB of public USGS airborne lidar from 118 campaigns to produce a common terrain/ecosystem framework. The detailed guide gives valid coverage of 331,804 square kilometres, about 78.3% of California land. The NASA report's Santa Clara County illustration uses 2020 observations at that particular location.

One-metre grid cells describe horizontal resolution. Bare-ground terrain, upper surfaces and canopy-height representations answer distinct questions. Richer vegetation statistics, acquisition-year and building-fraction layers appear at 10/30 metres. Year metadata identify older observations. The guide also describes gaps, interpolation/flightline artifacts and possible residual rooftops; assessing those at a specific site calls for inspection of local data.

The documented advance is a consistent research/planning resource. Wildfire, habitat and management applications are intended uses whose real effects need evaluation. Educational analogies and planning examples are explanatory analysis. Independent raw-tile and downstream outcome checks remain further work. The cover is a generated concept; source media stays linked.

Sources: [NASA, November 18](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/science-news/2026/11/18/california-wildfire-risk-3d-maps/), [ORNL DAAC catalog](https://daacweb-prod.ornl.gov/cgi-bin/dsviewer.pl?ds_id=2523), [dataset guide](https://daacweb-prod.ornl.gov/CMS/guides/Lidar_Topgraphy_EcoStructure.html), [USGS background](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-difference-between-lidar-data-and-a-digital-elevation-model-dem).
