# The service delivered and the energy lost

Source review, November 13, 2026. Rosenow and colleagues published The primary energy fallacy in energy transitions in npj Clean Energy on November 12. Their stylised counterfactual compares the 2022 global energy baseline with a highly electrified, renewable-supplied configuration delivering the same useful energy. Residual fossil uses remain; this is a thought experiment rather than a forecast or full net-zero pathway.

Researchers estimate a 61% reduction in rejected energy, while conventional primary-energy accounting yields a 48% reduction, or 288 exajoules annually. These measures have different denominators. Useful services stay fixed by assumption; neither result measures household bills, carbon emissions or observed 2026 savings. Heat pumps bring ambient heat into the balance, and more efficient generation and end uses reduce losses.

The result depends on technology and accounting assumptions. The generation configuration replaces nuclear and bioenergy as well as fossil generation, a scenario choice rather than a recommendation to close nuclear plants. Aggregated storage losses are included, while chronological grid reliability, demand growth, transition timing, costs and several material and land constraints remain outside the detailed model. Physical efficiency and practical delivery remain separate questions.
