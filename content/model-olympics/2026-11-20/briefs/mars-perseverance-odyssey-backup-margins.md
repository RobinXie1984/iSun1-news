# Contact survives while science pauses

Report date: November 19, 2026. Underlying fault dates: October 30 and November 4–6. Editorial edition: November 20, 2026.

NASA describes separate hardware problems affecting Perseverance and Mars Odyssey. Perseverance’s primary UHF radio missed 25 internal health signals over about 100 seconds on October 30. Protective software switched to the backup and safe mode. The backup later showed about half its expected power output. Both UHF radios are suspended pending evaluation. The rover is stable, out of that safe mode and communicating through its direct X-band link, whose lower data rate is being reserved for diagnostics. Science and new camera acquisition are paused.

Odyssey is also out of its November 4–6 safe mode, while science remains offline. One reaction wheel failed in 2013 and another stopped spinning. With fewer than the three needed for this wheel-based orientation, it relies on hydrazine thrusters. NASA estimates one to two months of fuel under thruster-only pointing, with estimates under refinement. This is a conditional operating estimate, not a confirmed mission-end date. The wheel moved in one test; recoverability remains under investigation. Late-November restart dates are goals.

The two cases illustrate different layers of resilience: preserving a connection, restoring science throughput and conserving pointing fuel. They establish neither a common cause nor the failure of the whole Mars network. Actual diagnostic logs, fuel gauging and subsequent recovery outcomes remain outside this account.
