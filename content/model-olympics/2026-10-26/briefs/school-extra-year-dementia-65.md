# Frozen factual brief — schooling and later dementia diagnoses
Collection2026-10-26. Report/paper2026-10-23; policy1972,not a new school reform today.
Sources:
https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/extra-year-secondary-school-lowered-dementia-diagnoses-later-life
https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.2529506123
F1 NIH describes UK reform raising the school-leaving age from15to16 for the affected birth cohort. Researchers studied over100,000UKBiobank participants; the reform increased staying to16 by14%. Do not relabel14% as dementia reduction.
F2 Barcellos and colleagues use a regression-discontinuity natural experiment, not random assignment or a simple graduate/non-graduate comparison. The paper argues a causal effect of education on fewerAD/ADRD diagnoses.
F3 Ascertainment used medical histories,hospitalrecords and deathregistries. Diagnoses beforeDecember2022 were assessed; oldestparticipants65. This is not lifetime prevention or evidence that adult evening classes reproduce the effect.
F4 The reported effect was strongest among higher genetic-risk participants. Genes are not destiny; this does not erase inherited risk,reverse disease or prove a specific biological mechanism.
F5 NIH's.88% wording is insufficient here to establish denominator/absolute-versus-relative estimand; omit that number and invented relative reductions. Socioeconomic/health pathways are possibilities,not established single causes. Fullpaper text unavailable in this check; abstract andNIHrelease reviewed.
F6 Positive: policy-based evidence suggests education may influence later brainhealth. Interpretation depends on design assumptions,selectedcohort,young endpoint and generalisability; no individual medical promise or advice. Preserve limitations prominently.
