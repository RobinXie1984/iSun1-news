The strategic appeal is understandable. Having multiple usable transportation options can provide flexibility when circumstances change. It can give planners more ways to meet a requirement and more room to respond when one route becomes unavailable.

But the word “usable” carries weight.

A spacecraft under development and a spacecraft certified for crew missions occupy different positions in a transportation system. The future value of an additional option depends on completing the work that makes it an actual option.

This is the tension at the center of NASA’s plan. The agency sees value in the capability Starliner could provide. The program still has to demonstrate that capability to the required standard.

Strategic importance explains why an organization might continue the effort. It cannot settle the engineering questions.
