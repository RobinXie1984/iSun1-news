# Starliner: 61 recommendations, then a 2028 crew-return plan
Original event/report date: 2026-09-28. Fact check: 2026-09-30 Hong Kong time.

[c1 / s1] NASA administrator Jared Isaacman announced the current plan to return astronauts on Boeing's Starliner-2 by 2028. Before that, uncrewed Starliner-1 could fly in December 2026 or January 2027 to evaluate engineering changes.
[c2 / s1] NASA says its investigation into the 2024 crew flight identified 61 recommendations. Service-module thrusters operated outside engineering qualification; thermal conditions and design features contributed to performance problems. Boeing made thermal modifications. An additional valve redesign and other changes remain part of certification work.
[c3 / s1] NASA intends to exercise options for fifth and sixth Starliner station flights and work with Boeing and ULA to certify Vulcan after Atlas V's final flight. The stated reason is maintaining multiple crew-transport options.
[c4 / s2] NASA's FY2027 budget material says U.S. crew transportation currently uses SpaceX Crew Dragon alongside Russian Soyuz, while Starliner works toward certification.

Boundary: 2028 is a plan, not a certified flight or guarantee. Sixty-one recommendations are not 61 proven unresolved defects. More missions are intended options, not evidence they already flew. Do not invent costs, blame, astronauts' feelings, or claims that another provider has no competition.
