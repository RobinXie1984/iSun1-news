# The forecast that learns from other forecasts' mistakes

Report October 14, 2026; collected October 17. Source: MIT News reporting its team's research, not an independent operational audit. Positive advance: useful probabilistic prediction in a difficult planning window, not weather control.

W1. MIT reports an ML postprocessing wrapper that learns systematic errors in existing forecasts, improving probabilistic forecasts two to six weeks ahead. Judah Cohen is the MIT co-author; he credits collaborator Lester Mackey with the initial idea. The team spans MIT, Harvard, Toronto, IMPA, DTN, ECMWF and Microsoft.
W2. In the reported ECMWF competition last year, the winning submission combined ECMWF's model with another model, then applied the correction. MIT describes doubled predictive power in this subseasonal range and improvement over climatological averages. Do not convert this into a universal percentage accuracy, exact daily weather six weeks ahead, or proof chaos theory was overturned.
W3. MIT reports statistically significant improvement for extreme weather and improved land forecasts in most regions tested. The wrapper can be added to different models. Training used MIT Engaging; the resulting model does not require a supercomputer to run. Cohen describes it as open source and low cost; no verified dollar cost or operational rollout scale is supplied.
W4. Kenya's meteorological office has contacted Cohen about implementation; that is interest, not a completed national deployment or measured crop benefit. Earlier warning could help farming, energy and emergency preparation; lives saved, avoided losses and local Hong Kong skill are UNKNOWN. Texas's February 2021 storms illustrate stakes, not a retrospective proof that this tool would have prevented that disaster.
W5. The Nature paper is titled 'Advancing Subseasonal Forecasting with Machine Learning', DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-11126-x.
